# Public products and processes

This is analysis of what a product does, not access to its private systems. Use public pages,
published pricing, public docs, your own trial account and your own onboarding messages. Do not
invent conversion rates, revenue, team plans or hidden internal process from a landing page.

## Observe

Walk the product as a customer: discovery → signup → first value → payment → support → cancellation.
Capture dated pages, prices, checkout totals and emails you actually received. Record country,
account type and date; public prices and funnels change. Collect public docs, changelogs and
policies. A DNS record or job post can suggest a tech stack, but cannot prove the whole stack.

## Specify and test

- Build a journey map with input (user action), visible output, delay and exception at each step.
- Form hypotheses ('the trial requires a card', 'support responds in a day'), then test with your
  own account or a public source. A single observation is not a service-level guarantee.
- For a replicated workflow, make cases for happy path, cancellation, refund, retry, timeout,
  invalid data and regional variations. Check outcomes and messages, not just screen similarity.
- Keep pricing fidelity (exact currency, tax and tiers) separate from process fidelity (steps and
  timings). Human judgment remains necessary for product positioning and brand tone.

## Report

Provide a dated fact table with source links, hypotheses still open, a journey map and what would
need access to prove. If the original has no accessible ground truth for private outcomes, say
'not measured' instead of 99.9%. `web-unblock` can help read public pages; it does not turn a
private process into an observed fact.
