# The Sabri Suby method, adapted for 3x Agentic

Sources: his long YouTube videos (transcripts read Oct 24, 2026; notes in `references/youtube-notes/`), his sales letter (selllikecrazybook.com), the King Kong site and his "Godfather offer" article, and his X/TikTok posts (Oct 18, 2026 research). His numbers ($7.8B, 100+ team, ad budgets) are HIS claims. Never borrow them.

Read this file once per session. Then use the module templates.

---

## 1. The two skills: get attention, then convert it

"Master two skills forever: capturing attention and converting attention." (Give Me 27 Minutes … Digital Marketing)
- Attention = hooks, first lines, subject lines, the first 2 seconds of a video. See `hooks.md`.
- Conversion = the offer, the proof, the risk reversal, the clear next step. See `offer-stack.md`.
- "Every text, email, video and DM is an act of persuasion." (The One Skill …) So every piece of copy has one job and one ask.

## 2. Research before you write (Halo strategy, dream buyer)

"Be a student of the market: their hopes and dreams, pains and fears, what keeps them up at night."
1. Name the dream buyer: one type of business, one role (owner, office manager, ops lead).
2. Find their exact words. Read their reviews (Google, Yelp, Trustpilot), job ads, Reddit threads, Facebook groups and forum posts. Sabri calls this "forum foraging".
3. Mine competitor reviews for repeated complaints. Each repeated complaint is an angle. (How I use AI in my business: feed reviews to AI, ask for recurring themes, turn each theme into an angle.)
4. Find the task "nearest to money": the manual step that loses leads, bookings or cash (missed calls, slow replies, unchased invoices). Sabri's AI-business video calls this "bolt AI onto boring local businesses".
5. Write down one true detail per prospect before any outbound message.

## 3. The market pyramid (talk to the 97%)

Larger Market Formula: about 3% are ready to buy now, 17% are gathering information, 20% know they have a problem, 60% don't know yet. (If You Only Watch One Marketing Video …)
- The big players fight over the 3%. Build for the other 97%.
- So content teaches (the 97%) and outbound offers the audit (the 3% and the 17%).
- "Don't ask someone to buy until they're already sold." (X cheat code #12)

## 4. Lead with value: the HVCO (high-value content offer)

"Find what competitors charge for, give the information away free, then sell the implementation." (17 Years … 7 Truths)
- "Crack vs kryptonite": crack = free answers people want. Kryptonite = "talk to a salesperson". Lead with crack.
- For 3x Agentic the HVCO is the **free automation audit**: a short call plus a written plan the owner keeps. The plan must leave them better off even if they never buy.
- Optional second HVCO (John to build): a short plain-English report, e.g. "The boring tasks most small businesses can automate first". Sabri's spec: 5-15 pages, not promotional, answers the questions the market already asks.

## 5. The Godfather offer (an offer they can't refuse)

"A strong offer will succeed in spite of weak copy, but strong copy won't overcome a weak offer." Spend most of your effort on the offer.
Method: write the over-the-top version "that would give your lawyer sweats", then pull back to what you can really deliver. "If the offer doesn't keep the founder up at night, it's not strong enough."
His 7 parts (If You Only Watch One …) and how 3x Agentic fills them:
1. Rationale (why this offer exists): a builder does the audit, not a salesperson.
2. Value build: what the plan shows and what the build does, in their task words.
3. Price: John sets it. This skill never states a price.
4. Payment plan: 50% to start, 50% after they see it working.
5. Premiums: the written plan they keep; 6 months of fixes and upkeep.
6. Power guarantee: the three guarantees in `offer-stack.md`. No free build. No money-back promise.
7. Scarcity (must be real): up to 10 audits a week.

## 6. The 9 questions every sales message must answer

(If You Only Watch One Marketing Video …) Use for landing pages, VSLs and long emails:
1. Is this for me? 2. Why pay attention now? 3. Is this the exact fix I need? 4. What is new about it? 5. What is the proof? 6. How does it work? 7. What do others say? 8. What guarantee do I have? 9. What do you want me to do next?
Short pieces (a DM, a post) answer 1, 3 and 9 only.

## 7. Copy rules from his ads and emails

- "Don't make your ads look like ads." Posts, emails and DMs should look like a person wrote them. Plain text. No banners.
- Personal-looking email: lower-case subject, no design, a human sender. "If somebody can smell from your subject line that your email is commercial, you're not going to get it opened."
- Email as an orchard: mostly value, a small pitch. (Digital Marketing Isn't Hard …)
- One big benefit plus intrigue in every hook ("hyper-dopamine" hook: a clear benefit and an open loop).
- Dog-whistle copy: specific enough that only the dream buyer keeps reading.
- The damaging admission: admit a real limit before you claim strength. For 3x Agentic: "We don't promise a number of hours saved before we see your process. We promise fixes for 6 months." (How to Sell ANYTHING at a Premium Price)
- Showmanship: show the hidden work. Screen-record the workflow; show the plan.

## 8. Channels for a founder with no ad budget

His blind ranking: Meta ads #1, cold email #2, cold calling #3 ("only requires sweat equity"), SEO #4, partnerships #5, organic content #6 ("like a slot machine"), referrals #7.
For John now: cold email + cold DMs first (free, direct). Content second, as proof.
- Content is a "force multiplier", not the engine. Prospects Google you before they buy: the "Google sniff test". Make a small set of deep, useful posts that prove you are real. (Digital Marketing Isn't Hard …; I Ranked the Best Client Getting Funnels)
- Prospect by hand first. Pick 10 businesses, research each one, write each message by hand. Then scale what works. (How I'd Make $10K/Month With No Product)
- "Sawdust method": find an asset the business throws away (old leads nobody followed up, enquiries that got no reply, old customers nobody asked back). Offer an automation that turns it into revenue. Strong cold angle.

## 9. Funnels (when there is traffic)

His ranking for a service business: an ungated VSL page with a booking button (A-tier), then a "bridge page" after booking (a short video + proof) so people show up ready. The gated VSL with fast phone follow-up is his S-tier, but it needs ad money and a setter. (I Ranked the Best Client Getting Funnels)
"Use a VSL at every step": one to book, one to make them show up, one for no-shows and non-buyers, one after purchase.

## 10. The sales call: diagnose like a doctor

(Learn 97% of Sales in 52 Minutes)
- "The deal closes at discovery." Listen most of the call. Use their exact words.
- "One feeds two": after the diagnosis, a short demo (under 2 minutes) of a workflow like theirs.
- Cost of inaction: let them say what staying the same costs. Don't invent the number.
- "Sales aikido": agree, then go back to the diagnosis. "Winning arguments doesn't lead to deals."
- Find the silent objection: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you?" "What would make it a 10?"
- Offer up to three options. Build value first, state the price, then stay quiet.
- Push for yes or no. "Maybe" kills more deals than "no". But never fake urgency.
- "Value is the antidote to skepticism."

## 11. Follow-up

"Fortunes are made on follow-up." Count your follow-up touches today, then plan to triple them (17 Years … 7 Truths). Each touch adds value or new information. "People don't need more time. They need more information."

## 12. Track the inputs every day

Track leading numbers daily, not results monthly: emails sent, DMs sent, replies, audits booked, audits held, plans sent. Fix the input before a bad month happens. (Learn 97% of Sales …)
Protect "money hours": a fixed daily block for only revenue work (outreach, follow-ups, calls, content). (Success Is Hard Until You Build Systems …)

## 13. Engineer the second sale

"Don't optimize the first sale for max profit. Use it to open the door to the next one." (How To Charge 30x More …)
For 3x Agentic: the audit leads to the first build. The 6-month care cover keeps John in touch, so the next build is a natural question. The audit plan should always name "what to automate next".

## 14. More tools from the videos

- **Name the method** ("hero mechanism", from The $25B Strategy and his landing-page course): say why other fixes fail, why ours works, and give it a name. Draft for 3x Agentic: "Boring Task First": we map the task before we pick the tool. John approves the name before public use.
- **Founder as proof** (The $25B Strategy): John's real story of why he builds automations beats any claim. Ask John for it; never invent it.
- **Application, not "book a call"** (6 Dark Marketing Tactics): a short pre-call form ("what do you do by hand today? what have you tried?") makes the audit feel earned and gives John the diagnosis before the call.
- **Three options** (decoy pricing): offer small, medium and full builds so the question becomes "which one", not "yes or no". John sets every price.
- **Remove friction** (one-click effect): e-signature and a payment link instead of printed forms.
- **Entry offer** (splinter offer): the free audit is the first, easiest yes. It must be better than what others charge for.
- **Speed to lead** (Everyone Who Uses This Strategy …): he contacts new leads within seconds on every channel. For John this is both a habit (reply to every audit request fast) and a product to sell (lead-reply automations).
- **Mine reviews for angles** (his landing-page course, "The $1 Million Landing Page Strategy"; How I use AI): pull 2-4 star reviews of the prospect's competitors and the longest forum posts; list the pains, fears, hopes and exact phrases; each repeated theme is an angle.
- **Comparative angle** (The $25B Strategy): read competitors' 1-star reviews and say plainly how we avoid those flaws, only when true.
- **Work in "money hours"** (Success Is Hard … Systems; Top 0.01%): a fixed daily block for outreach, follow-ups and calls. Default to "no" for anything that doesn't move the one priority.

## 15. What we do NOT copy

- His proof numbers, team size, money talk, luxury flexing, hidden-camera shopping, swearing.
- "Disgustingly rich" money hype and anything on John's banned list.
- Fake scarcity ("stock is low") and made-up deadlines.
- Money-back or "we work until you hit X" promises. John's three guarantees only.
- Pricing advice that assumes a known brand. John sets prices; this skill does not.
