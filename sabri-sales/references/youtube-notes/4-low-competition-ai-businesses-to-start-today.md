## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby argues the winning AI businesses in 2026 aren't "AI businesses" per se — they're old, proven business models (ad creative, local service, ecom, affiliate content) where AI strips out cost, time, and team bloat while a human still owns strategy, taste, and offer. The edge isn't the tool, it's picking a proven, already-in-demand niche/offer and using AI to execute it faster and cheaper than competitors can.

4. **Bolt-on AI to boring local businesses** — Pick a durable local service business (plumbers, dentists, cleaners) with proven demand, then attack their nearest-to-money pain point: missed calls/missed revenue.

6. Prospect via scraping (he names Apollo to pull Google My Business contacts) and cold email with a specific, novel angle (not generic "marketing help") — reference a real pain point ("I tried calling you last week, no one picked up").
7. Pitch economics as replacement + upside: compare the $1-2k/mo AI service to a $50-70k/year front-desk hire, plus the revenue currently lost from missed after-hours calls.
8. **AI-enhanced ecommerce brand** — Validate the market first using data tools (he names TikTok Shop data, Kalodata-type tools, Amazon tool "Import Genie") before picking a product; only enter categories with provable revenue/ad-spend scaling.
9. Use AI to clone winning ad angles/hooks, auto-build the Shopify store, product photos, descriptions, and video (he names Replo), then run AI-driven split tests that auto-optimize layout/copy for conversion.
10. **AI TikTok Shop affiliate** — Pick an already-proven, high-repeat-purchase product using data tools and TikTok Creative Studio; find top organic content with a tool (he names a Chrome extension "Sort") sorted by views/engagement.
11. Pull transcripts of proven videos, rewrite 2-3 script variations modeling the same hooks, generate a consistent AI influencer (same Midjourney-generated face) to deliver them, and post 3-6x/day to find winners through volume.
12. Treat each business as a fast test, not a forever commitment: "it's movement, not meditation" — start now, expect to iterate or pivot within months.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Lead cold outreach with a specific, named pain point (e.g., missed calls, slow lead follow-up) rather than generic "AI/automation services" — matches his "new and novel, not generic marketing" angle.
- Offer a concrete, bounded deliverable with a clear timeframe (e.g., "a working voice agent demo in X days") instead of vague ongoing retainers — mirrors his "48-hour, 25-30 creatives" clarity.
- Build a live, personalized demo for prospects (customized to their business name/voice) before asking for commitment — this is honest because they see the real product working, not a promise.
- Frame pricing around real, verifiable costs the business already knows (their current staffing/tool spend) rather than invented industry stats — only cite stats you can actually source or caveat as estimates.
- Pick an audience with provable, repeat need for your service (e.g., businesses that already get many calls/leads) so retention is real, not something propped up by hype.
- Use AI to produce your own outbound/content assets (scripts, demo videos) faster and cheaper, freeing time for the actual strategic/taste work — the "AI does execution, you do strategy" split applies directly to agency ops.
- Validate before building: check that a prospect actually has the pain point (e.g., ask about current call handling) before pitching, instead of assuming demand.
- Treat early offers as fast tests to iterate on, not final products — ship a version this week, refine based on real client response.

- Volume-based content strategy (posting 3-6x/day, testing many products) depends on his team's bandwidth/tools; a tiny agency should adapt cadence to what's sustainable, not copy volume claims as a requirement for success.
