## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby argues a business's growth is capped by the founder's own decisions, not external circumstances — every bottleneck is "a you problem." His seven truths move from personal responsibility, to planning with the end in mind, to targeting the 97% of the market not ready to buy yet with free value, to systematic follow-up, to scaling distribution, to knowing true unit economics, and finally to focusing on lifetime value over acquisition cost.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **"If it's meant to be, it's up to me"** — treat every business problem (sales, hiring, culture) as a personal/founder problem you control.
2. **"Who Not How"** — when stuck wearing all the hats, ask who to hire, not how to do it yourself; hire a team collectively better than you.
3. **Annual planning loop** — review last year's topline/bottom-line and input metrics → define what next 12 months must look like → break into quarterly, weekly, daily roadmap.
4. **Top-3 vs. 97% framing** — identify the 3 biggest competitors ("gorillas") who chase the 3% ready-to-buy now; instead build for the other 97% still researching.
5. **High-Value Content Offer** — find what competitors charge for, give the information away free, then sell implementation.
6. **"Crack vs. Kryptonite"** — crack = free information/answers people actually want; kryptonite = forcing them to "talk to a salesperson." Lead with crack.
7. **Lead magnet spec** — build a 5–15 page non-promotional report answering the market's "hair on fire" questions; must leave the prospect better off regardless of purchase.
8. **Bridge step before follow-up** — insert a VSL/webinar between the free content and the sales follow-up to move people from "who are you" to ready-to-buy.
9. **Triple the follow-up** — audit CRM for current follow-up count per lead, then write a plan to triple it.
10. **X-ray / closed-loop tracking** — track cohorts of when people actually buy (7/14/28/90+ days) to justify longer nurture and follow-up investment.
11. **Unit economics first** — calculate true LTV (last 12 months revenue ÷ customers), then decide max allowable spend to acquire a customer before touching ads.
12. **80/20 flip on LTV** — spend 80% of effort improving stickiness/LTV (not just lowering CAC); use leading indicators of churn (missed calls, unopened emails) as intervention triggers.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Treat agency bottlenecks (slow pipeline, inconsistent outreach) as founder/process problems to fix systematically, not bad luck — audit your own cold email/DM cadence before blaming the channel.
- Build a free "high-value content offer" for small-business owners: a short, real PDF/video like "5 Processes You Can Automate This Month" that gives genuine answers, not a disguised pitch.
- Target the 97%-not-ready-to-buy SMB owners (via X/LinkedIn/cold DM educational content) instead of only chasing the few actively searching "AI automation agency now."
- Insert a bridge step (a short demo video or mini case-study walkthrough) between your free content and a sales call, so prospects self-qualify before talking to you.
- Commit to tripling follow-up sequences on inbound leads/replies — track in a CRM how many touches you currently do and build a realistic cadence instead of a 2-3 touch dropout.
- Calculate real unit economics: what a client is worth over their lifetime (repeat builds, retainers) before deciding how much time/ad/DM-outreach effort is worth spending to land them.
- Shift effort toward retention and expansion (more automations, maintenance retainers) once a client is live — watch for leading churn signals like missed check-ins, instead of only chasing new leads.
- Keep offers and outreach honest and concrete — give real examples/answers for free, and let the quality of that free value (not hype) build trust for paid builds.

## Do not copy

- Any specific revenue/leads numbers he cites ($250M, $7.8B, 10x leads, 25–50% conversion rates) are his own unverified claims — don't borrow them as facts.
- His ability to run 16–18 hour days, live on $36k/year for 3 years, or self-fund with 30-day credit-card float assumes personal runway a tiny agency owner may not have — don't assume this is replicable or advisable without caution.
- Large ad budgets and paid traffic scaling (Meta/Google ads, "pump $10k into ads") depend on capital a small agency likely doesn't have; cold email/DM/organic content are the realistic substitute, not large ad spend.
- His "Shark Tank" fame and brand recognition lend credibility his content gets attention partly because of pre-existing audience/authority — a new agency can't borrow that trust and must earn it purely through demonstrated value.
