## Core idea

Attention doesn't make you money — funnels and economics do. Any creator or business with traffic needs three things: attention, a funnel that warms up cold traffic, and an irresistible offer that turns strangers into buyers, with a cheap "splinter" front-end offer that sets up a bigger back-end sale.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **The Three Things You Need**: (1) attention (earned or bought), (2) a funnel — a step-by-step process that warms up cold traffic and educates them, (3) an offer compelling enough to convert "who the hell are you" into "shut up and take my money." [2:07]
2. **Godfather Offer**: craft an offer "so good that your prospects simply can't refuse it," built around a cheap, low-risk entry point. [3:35]
3. **Entry/Splinter Offer**: a very small, cheap offer (he uses $7) that gets an easy yes, solves one "micro pressing problem," and proves your stuff works fast. [4:30]
4. **Engineered Path / Second Sale**: the first sale's job is to set up the second sale — call or follow up with buyers/members and pitch a higher-ticket offer (he suggests ~$5K coaching). [4:55]
5. **Bridge Page**: instead of sending cold traffic straight to a thin landing page, send them to a long-form page that further warms them up before the pitch. [7:26]
6. **VSL / Mini-Documentary**: build a well-crafted video sales letter (20–30 min) telling the founder's story emotionally plus proof — many testimonials (video, written, screenshots) — to do the heavy lifting of persuasion. [8:10]
7. **Survey the Audience**: use a simple Google Form/SurveyMonkey to ask your audience what they want before building the splinter product, instead of guessing. [5:35]
8. **Liquidate Ad Spend on Front End, Profit on Back End**: the front-end/splinter offer should roughly cover acquisition cost; the real profit comes from the second and third offers after delivery cost. [10:37]

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Offer a genuinely small, low-risk "splinter" entry product (e.g., a one-page automation audit or a single quick-win workflow build) priced to cover acquisition cost, not to maximize first-sale profit.
- Make the first deliverable fast and real — a working micro-automation that proves you deliver — before pitching the larger build/retainer.
- Build a short case-study/proof page (your "bridge page") for cold DM/email/X traffic before sending them to a booking link — don't ask cold strangers to "buy my stuff" directly.
- Use actual client testimonials, before/after screenshots, and short case videos as proof — not manufactured urgency or fake scarcity.
- Survey your audience/prospects directly (DMs, forms) to find out which specific automation problem they want solved before building the splinter offer.
- Design an honest next step: audit → small paid pilot automation → larger ongoing build/retainer, each step earning the right to the next.
- Track real conversion math (leads → calls → pilots → retainers) instead of assuming big numbers; don't invent industry benchmarks you haven't verified for your own niche.
- Keep the back-end offer (retainer/bigger build) as where real profit is made, after the front-end pilot has covered your outreach/time cost.

## Do not copy

- The 1% "industry benchmark" conversion rate and the $8.5M projection are his own hypothetical/claimed figures for someone else's business — don't present unverified industry stats as fact.
- He has Shark Tank fame, huge audience reach, and can speculate about someone else's revenue/numbers; small agencies shouldn't publicly "estimate" or call out competitors' numbers without data.
- The large ad budgets, VSL production budgets (20–30 min documentaries), and call-center-style manual follow-up ("call all of those people") assume resources and team size a 3-person agency may not have — scale tactics down accordingly.
- Any implied scarcity/urgency language ("spots are limited," "we hit capacity") he himself criticizes as "fake-ass scarcity" — don't imitate what he's mocking.
