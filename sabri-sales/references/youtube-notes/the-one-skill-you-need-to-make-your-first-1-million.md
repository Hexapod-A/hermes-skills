## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sales is just one branch of the master skill: persuasion. Persuasion isn't manipulation — it's giving someone clarity and conviction to take an action that's already in their own best interest, by channeling desire that already exists in the market rather than pitching product features. In the AI era, where anyone can build a product, the ability to persuade (and thus distribute) becomes the real differentiator.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **Reframe sales as persuasion** — every text, email, video, DM is an act of persuasion; you don't get to opt out since all communication asks something of someone.
2. **Channel existing desire, don't invent it** — ask what the market's pains, fears, hopes, and dreams are rather than listing features/benefits.
3. **Define the prospect's dream destination** — identify where they want to go in life/business, then position your product as the vehicle to get them there.
4. **Shift messaging focus** — put the prospect's end outcome at the center of all messaging, not your product on a pedestal.
5. **Get volume (High-Frequency Sales Job)** — in your 20s, take a high-volume, low-ticket sales role to rack up reps fast; volume builds skill.
6. **Do the YAP Challenge** — daily for 365 days: get a random topic from AI, research 10 minutes, put notes away, talk uninterrupted to camera educating a viewer with a hot take. Builds coherent communication (the mouth).
7. **Pair yapping with asking** — separately build the "nerve" muscle via negotiating or asking strangers for money/rejection; yapping alone isn't enough.
8. **Persist in the ask** — within a sales conversation, keep asking for the close multiple times rather than giving up after one no.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Stop pitching automation features (n8n flows, AI agents); lead with the business owner's actual pain — time lost, missed leads, manual busywork — and the outcome they want (more booked jobs, less admin).
- In cold email/DM copy, frame the automation as the vehicle to their "dream destination" (e.g., a shop running itself) rather than a technical product.
- Use volume deliberately: a high number of cold outreach touches (emails, DMs) is how you build pattern-recognition for what messaging actually lands with small-business owners.
- Apply the YAP Challenge format to content: daily short videos/X posts where you take one automation-related topic and give a clear, researched hot take — builds on-camera/writing reps for audience-building.
- Practice persistence honestly: follow up multiple times on a warm lead or reply, not through fake urgency, but through genuinely restating the value and asking clearly for a next step (audit call, demo).
- Build the "asking" muscle by doing real discovery calls — ask directly about budget, decision process, and pain, even when it's uncomfortable, instead of avoiding the close.
- Keep the psychology constant (clarity, pain-to-outcome mapping) even as tools/platforms (cold email, DMs, LinkedIn) change month to month.
