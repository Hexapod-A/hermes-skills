## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby breaks down how the beauty brand Asop charges roughly 30x more than competitors for hand soap, not through celebrity endorsements or discounts, but through a deliberate system: using an everyday ritual as the entry product, running a diagnosis instead of a hard sell, engineering the second sale, and using packaging as a walking billboard. The underlying lesson is that small/new businesses can't outspend incumbents on advertising, so they should compete on positioning, experience, and lifetime value engineering instead.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **Zig when everybody else zags** — Identify what the dominant players in your category all do (discounts, celebrities, big-ticket occasions), then do the opposite deliberately. [1:29]
2. **Ritual economics** — Find the thing people in your category do very often (the "10x per day hand wash" of your industry) and turn that commodity moment into a premium product/offer. [4:09]
3. **Sell like a doctor** — Train your sales process around diagnosis (ask about their situation, categorize their need) rather than a pitch; lead with curiosity questions instead of "can I help you?" [6:16-7:18]
4. **Engineer the second sale** — Don't optimize the first transaction for max profit; use it to open the door to upsells (free samples, follow-up touchpoints) so the first sale creates the question "what's next?" [9:23-9:50]
5. **Design as a differentiator** — Invest in packaging/experience (even at higher cost) so customers want to keep, reuse, or photograph and share it, turning the product itself into free marketing. [10:27-11:50]

## Use for an AI automation agency

- **Ritual economics for agencies**: identify the repetitive task owners already do constantly (e.g., manually answering the same customer questions, copy-pasting leads into a spreadsheet) and build your entry offer around automating that specific daily friction point.
- **Sell like a doctor, not a vendor**: run a real discovery/audit call that diagnoses their actual workflow bottlenecks before proposing anything — ask about their current process, tools, and pain, rather than opening with a pitch deck.
- **Entry offer as on-ramp**: price a small, low-risk first build (e.g., one automated workflow) as the "hand soap" — affordable enough to say yes to, valuable enough to prove you're worth the next, bigger engagement.
- **Engineer the second sale honestly**: after delivering the first automation, show the client where it naturally creates the next need (e.g., automating lead intake naturally surfaces a need for automated follow-up) — don't invent needs, point out real ones your work revealed.
- **Design as differentiator in deliverables**: make your audit reports, Loom walkthroughs, and documentation clean and shareable so clients want to show them to other business owners — this is honest, zero-cost word-of-mouth.
- **Zig vs. the agency crowd**: if most AI agencies lead with hype/fake case studies in cold DMs, differentiate by leading with a concrete, specific audit of one real inefficiency you noticed in their business.
- **Lifetime value over one-off builds**: frame pricing and relationship around ongoing maintenance/expansion of automations rather than treating each build as a single closed transaction.

## Do not copy

- The celebrity/L'Oreal acquisition narrative and $3.7B valuation — this is brand-scale fame irrelevant to a tiny agency's cold outreach.
- His own claims about running a "$250 million agency" and being a "Shark Tank" shark — credibility built on pre-existing fame/money that a new agency doesn't have.
- The physical retail sensory experience (store fit-out, incense, tea, perfume spritzing) — not transferable to cold email/DM/LinkedIn motion; don't force a "whole store experience" metaphor onto a B2B service.
- Any specific stats (70% revenue left on table, 10x more people prefer consults, "hundreds of thousands" of book copies) — these are his unverified claims, not data to repeat as fact in your own materials.
- The implication that premium pricing alone (30x) is achievable from packaging/vibe — for a B2B automation seller, pricing power has to come from real, demonstrated ROI, not aesthetic signaling.
