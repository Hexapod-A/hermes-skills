## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby's method is to never start AI from a blank page — instead you feed it massive, specific context (raw transcripts, tickets, reviews, winning ad data) and give it a narrow, high-value question to dig through. The value isn't "write me an ad," it's using AI to do the exhaustive data-mining (listening to thousands of calls, reading thousands of reviews) that would otherwise take a human months or years, so you can find the real constraint in the business.

## Frameworks and steps (numbered)

1. **Pick one constraint/pain point first** — don't apply AI randomly; find the specific business problem (e.g., sales dipped for 2.5 months) before touching a tool.
2. **Pull the raw data into one context window** — e.g., every sales call transcript for 18 months, or every customer support ticket, loaded into one Claude project.
3. **Assign AI a specific expert role with a specific task** — "act as a sales manager, rank every rep on dials, talk time, script adherence, objection handling."
4. **Get a full trend/scorecard breakdown** — month-by-month trends, word clouds, recurring themes, commitment scores, close-rate patterns — not just a single answer.
5. **For marketing angles:** identify top competitors → feed AI their Trust Pilot/Google reviews → ask it to find recurring themes → categorize into distinct ad angles.
6. **Never ask AI to write ads cold** — give it your own proven winning ad framework/format and your top 100 headlines, and tell it to write new ads/headlines in that style so no one could tell it's AI-written.
7. **Test in volume, then scale the winner** — run multiple angles/headlines as static ads in a CBO, find the winning angle, then turn that into a 2–3 minute VSL ad.
8. **Reuse the same data-mining process** across emails, landing page FAQs, and support content — mine the same ticket/review data for new copy.
9. **Make it a recurring workflow** — build the process once, then have it run weekly so you always have a pulse on sales calls, objections, and competitor mentions.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Before pitching a prospect, mine their own data (reviews, support tickets, website copy, any call recordings they'll share) instead of guessing at their pain points — give AI real context, not a blank-page prompt.
- When writing cold emails/DMs, don't ask AI to "write a cold email" cold — feed it a swipe file of your own best-performing outreach and real evidence from the prospect's business (reviews, LinkedIn posts, job listings) to personalize it.
- Apply his "rank and trend" approach internally: record your own sales/discovery calls, transcribe them, and have AI score them on objection handling, close rate, talk-time ratio — use this to actually improve your pitch, not fabricate stats.
- For case-study/content creation, mine your own support tickets or client Slack threads for real recurring objections and turn those into honest FAQ content or video topics — not invented pain points.
- Use the "test several angles small, scale the winner" logic for organic content: post multiple honest value-prop variations (short videos, LinkedIn posts) and double down on what actually gets engagement — don't rely on one unverified angle.
- Treat AI as a force-multiplier for the unglamorous grunt work (reading every lead reply, categorizing objections, summarizing call notes) so a tiny team can compete on diligence, not on a fake "we have a huge team" claim.
- When offering an "AI audit" to a small business, it's honest to frame it exactly as this video does: find one real constraint in their operation, show what deep data analysis reveals, not a vague "AI will transform your business" pitch.
