## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Ads perform best when they don't look like ads: unbranded "phantom" pages, raw camera-roll creative, and story-driven copy outperform polished branded content because they blend into native feed content people already consume. Winning ads die from obsessing over the wrong variable (creative fatigues before copy), so the fix is systematic iteration (new creative/headlines/body copy) and disciplined, data-driven patience (7-day rolling averages) rather than daily panic-killing.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **The Phantom Page** — Run ads from an unbranded personal/founder page instead of the polished company page; move your winning brand-page ad over unchanged first, then build founder-story ads from that page.
2. **The Trojan Story** — Write a mass-market story (grab ideas from Reddit) with a bold, gossip-like opening line; only connect it to your product/offer near the end, then make a soft call to action.
3. **The Slippery Lead-in** — Rewrite the first 11-14 words of your ad as a conversational, quotation-marked line that drops the reader into the high point of the story (e.g., a testimonial), not a generic pitch opener.
4. **The Hyperdopamine Ad checklist** — Model ads on what's already most-consumed on the platform (news/gossip format): does it (a) interrupt pattern, (b) open a curiosity loop, (c) state a hyperspecific big benefit.
5. **The Velvet CTA** — Default to "Learn More" instead of "Shop Now"; it's a low-commitment ask that funnels more cold clicks to the landing page, which does the actual selling.
6. **The ROAS Barbell** — Only run two creative types: zero-effort raw camera-roll statics, or maximum-effort high-production shoots; avoid the mediocre middle (generic UGC/branded statics).
7. **Zombie Campaign** — Take the 80-90% of ads Meta won't spend on (losers by spend, not necessarily by quality) and relaunch them in their own CBO/dedicated budget to force spend and surface hidden winners.
8. **Never Kill a Dying Ad** — When a winner fatigues, refresh in order: new creative first (fatigues fastest), then new headlines, then new body copy variations — before assuming the ad is dead.
9. **Fatigue-check framework (3 steps)** — Use 7-day rolling averages (not daily data); check account-wide top-of-funnel metrics (CPM/CTR) for external causes; check frequency (under 3 = not fatigued, above 3 doesn't automatically mean dead).

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Post from a **founder's personal profile** (not a faceless "3x Agentic" brand page) on X/LinkedIn/DMs — people engage with a person building in public, not a logo.
- Use **real, raw screen-recordings or camera-roll style content** (actual automation dashboards, actual client Slack messages) instead of polished stock-style graphics — authenticity over polish.
- Apply the **Trojan Story** honestly: tell a real story about a specific business problem you solved (e.g., "This plumber was losing 10 leads a week to missed calls") before pitching the audit/build — no fabricated gossip hooks, just a true before/after.
- Rewrite cold email/DM **opening lines** to drop prospects into a concrete, specific result or problem statement instead of "Hi, do you want to save time with automation?" — but only use real, verifiable numbers from actual clients.
- Favor a **soft CTA** in cold outreach and posts ("see how it works," "take a look") over "book a call now" — let the landing page/VSL do the selling after curiosity is earned.
- Apply the **barbell approach to content**: either quick, no-edit raw videos/screen recordings showing a real automation, or occasionally a genuinely high-effort case-study video — skip the mediocre middle of generic AI-template content.
- When testing outreach scripts or ad copy, **don't abandon a message after one weak day** — track performance over a rolling window before concluding it doesn't work, and iterate the opening line/hook before scrapping the whole approach.
- Build landing pages/VSLs that function like the "advertorial" he describes: lead with a real story/problem, build context, then make the ask — rather than a hard sell up front.

## Do not copy

- Any reliance on his **$10.2B / $300M ad spend** claims as "proof" — a tiny agency has no comparable budget or dataset to make such claims, and shouldn't imply it does.
- **Fabricated or gossip-bait hooks** (e.g., "I walked in on my wife cheating") used purely to manipulate curiosity with no real connection — a small honest agency should use true stories only, not shock-bait unrelated to the offer.
- Paid **ad-account tactics requiring real ad spend** (zombie campaigns, CBO budget allocation, scaling via Meta spend) — these need an active, funded ad account 3x Agentic likely doesn't have in cold outreach/organic contexts.
- Large-scale **creative production** ("monster high-production ads," week-long shoots, actors, exotic locations) — not feasible or necessary for a small outbound-focused agency.
- Treating **"Learn More" CTA optimization and ROAS/frequency thresholds** as directly applicable — these are paid-media-specific metrics; cold email/DM/organic content doesn't have the same mechanics and shouldn't borrow the exact numeric thresholds (e.g., frequency >3) without a paid ad context.
