## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Digital marketing success rests on two permanent skills: capturing attention and converting that attention into sales. Sabri Suby teaches building these skills by studying proven ad swipe files, building an industry-specific swipe file, deeply researching customer pains/fears/desires, then assembling copy with a repeatable 17-step framework — while constantly testing and refining.

## Frameworks and steps (numbered)

1. **Understand the foundation** — Master two skills forever: capturing attention and converting attention. [1:02]
2. **Capture attention via swipe files** — Study 1960s-80s direct response print ads daily; build a swipe file for 30 days. [2:06]
3. **Convert attention / pick traffic type** — Demand capture (existing search demand, "bleeding neck" prospects) vs. demand generation (create desire via ads); big money is in demand gen. [3:09]
4. **Study modern media (ADA framework)** — Make ads look like native content (news/findings/gossip); structure: Attention, Desire, Action (he says "ADA" though lists Attention/Interest/Desire/Action). [5:15]
5. **Build your industry swipe file** — New social account, follow niche brands, screenshot recurring ads, check Meta Ads Library for long-running ads, log competitor funnels/landing pages, research revenue via AI tools. [8:24]
6. **The Halo Strategy** — Find your "coliseum keyword" (highest-traffic term in your niche), mine Reddit/Quora for emotionally charged pains/fears/desires, populate a template. [9:26]
7. **Launch and learn** — Write 5 static ads/day, use broad targeting, start at ~$20/day (~$140/week) to test ~25 creatives; judge winners by ROAS (products) or cost-per-lead (leads) and CTR. [11:34]
8. **Market Sophistication & Competition Continuum** — 5 awareness states (most aware → unconsciously unaware); red ocean (aware, responds to price/offer) vs orange (problem/solution aware, responds to claims/proof) vs blue ocean (unaware, responds to story/education). [13:38]
9. **17-Step Secret Selling System** (for landing pages/funnels): (1) Call out audience, (2) Headline (big bold benefit), (3) Sub-headline, (4) Irresistible intrigue, (5) Spotlight the problem, (6) Reveal solution, (7) Credentials, (8) Benefits (not features), (9) Social proof, (10) Godfather offer (specific, can't-refuse), (11) Stack bonuses (2-3 sweet spot), (12) Stack value, (13) Reveal price, (14) Inject scarcity, (15) Powerful guarantee, (16) Clear CTA, (17) PS summary. [16:44]–[23:01]
10. **Dial in your funnel** — Track CTR, CPL, ROAS, time on site, conversion rate; form hypotheses and continuously split-test against controls. [25:08]
11. **Never stop learning** — Study every ad you see, even outside your niche, for transferable ideas. [26:11]

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Build a swipe file of cold emails/DMs and landing pages from other automation/AI agencies targeting SMBs — study what's running long (a proxy for "it's working"), not what looks flashy.
- Apply the Halo Strategy to your SMB niche: mine Reddit/Facebook groups/Google reviews for owners' actual pains around manual admin, missed leads, slow follow-up — don't guess their problems.
- Match messaging to awareness level honestly: most small-business owners are "problem aware" (know they're losing leads/time) not "solution aware" (don't know AI automation exists) — educate rather than assume they're ready to buy.
- Use benefits-over-features in outreach: instead of "we build n8n workflows," say "stop losing leads that don't get a reply for 3 days."
- Apply the 17-step structure to your landing page/VSL honestly: real credentials (actual builds you've done), real guarantees you can back, genuine bonuses — skip fabricated scarcity ("only 3 spots") unless literally true for your capacity.
- Test static "ad" style short-form posts/hooks before investing in polished video — get reps and feedback fast on which problem-framing resonates with SMB owners.
- Track real metrics (reply rate, call-booked rate, close rate) and treat each cold email/DM batch as a split test with a hypothesis, the same discipline he applies to paid ads.
- Keep assigning yourself "lifelong learner" status: study ads/outreach outside automation/SaaS for copy angles you can adapt to your niche.

## Do not copy

- His $7.8B in client sales and "Shark Tank" fame are used as authority/credibility — a new agency has no such proof and shouldn't claim it.
- The swipe-file approach assumes access to Meta Ads Library data at scale and money to buy $50-79 swipe file products — fine to adapt free, but don't treat paid ad research as mandatory.
- His recommended $20/day × 25 creatives testing budget assumes disposable ad spend most 3-person agencies cold-emailing/DMing won't have; cold outreach is the zero-budget equivalent, not a substitute for his paid-traffic framework.
- Scarcity/urgency tactics ("only 15 items left," arbitrary guarantees like "43% revenue increase") must only be used if literally true — inventing scarcity or unverified guarantees would violate the "no fake urgency" constraint.
- His claim that split-testing 5 ads nightly is required "to never go hungry" presumes an ad-based business model; a cold-outreach agency's equivalent volume/testing is different (emails, DMs) and shouldn't be force-fit to paid ad metrics (ROAS, CPL) that don't apply.
