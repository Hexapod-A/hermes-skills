## Core idea

Business success comes down to three numbers you must engineer in your favor: customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), and payback period. Whoever can profitably spend the most to acquire a customer wins the market, and that ability comes from better offers and messaging — not cheaper traffic.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **The three numbers** — CAC, LTV, payback period. Get the ratio and speed right and "there is no problem that you can't solve."
2. **He who spends most wins** — Treat all customer acquisition as paid (organic = paid with time). The business that can burden the highest CAC buys up the market.
3. **The equation for a good business** — LTV = revenue ÷ customers over 12-36 months. CAC = all costs tied to conversion. Target ratio: 3:1 LTV:CAC.
4. **Payback period** — How fast you recoup the $1 spent to acquire a customer; aim ~30 days (service) or ~90 days (ecom) so cash flow, not ROAS, funds growth. Use upsells/setup fees/one-click upsells to shorten it.
5. **Stop obsessing over the wrong number** — Raising LTV a few % beats shaving CAC 10-20%, since you can then afford to outspend everyone.
6. **Think like a billionaire (leverage)** — Spend 80% of time on revenue-producing "tip of the spear" activities: messaging/copy/creative/persuasion skill-building.
7. **Identify your dream buyer — Larger Market Formula** — 3% ready to buy, 17% information-gathering, 20% problem-aware, 60% not problem-aware. Market to the whole pyramid, not just the 3%.
8. **Halo Strategy / Forum Foraging** — Find the "bullseye keyword," mine forums/Reddit/reviews for exact customer language, then build the product/offer to match market desire (not what's convenient to sell).
9. **Capture leads with value-based marketing** — Use long-form copy, a "down and dirty PDF," or a video sales letter (VSL) to get name/email before selling. Apply the **NUIEPH** formula: messaging must be New, Unique, Exciting, Easy, Predictable, Huge.
10. **The Godfather Offer (7 parts)** — Rationale (why this offer can exist), Value build (anchor to "everyday price"), Price (lead with most aggressive price, profit on repeat sales), Payment plans, Premiums (give secrets away, sell implementation), Power Guarantee, Scarcity (must be real).
11. **The 9 questions every sales message must answer** — (1) Is this of personal interest to me? (2) Why pay attention now? (3) Is this the exact solution I need? (4) What's NUIEPH about it? (5) What's the proof? (6) How does it work? (7) What are others saying? (8) What guarantee do I have? (9) What do you want me to do next?

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Calculate real numbers for your own funnel: what does it cost you (time + tools + ad spend) to land one automation client (CAC), and what's a client worth over 12 months (LTV)? Don't guess — track it.
- Build a genuine "payback period" mindset: price builds/retainers so the first project pays back your outreach/time costs quickly (e.g., a paid audit or small build before a bigger retainer).
- Apply the Halo Strategy honestly: mine r/smallbusiness, Facebook groups, LinkedIn comments for how SMB owners actually describe their bottlenecks before writing cold emails/DMs — use their language, not jargon like "agentic workflows."
- Use the 9-question checklist on landing pages/VSLs for your audit offer: say exactly who it's for ("Attention plumbing business owners spending 10 hrs/week on manual scheduling"), show real proof (screenshots of time saved, actual client results), state a clear next step.
- Offer a real, honest guarantee tied to performance (e.g., "if the automation doesn't save you X hours/month, we keep working for free") rather than vague promises — this lowers perceived risk without fake claims.
- Use scarcity only if genuine (e.g., limited onboarding slots per month because you do builds manually) — don't fake countdown timers.
- Lead-magnet instead of pitching cold: offer a free, specific PDF/checklist or short automation audit video that solves one real problem (e.g., "3 workflows every HVAC company can automate this month") to earn contact info before pitching a paid build.
- Reinvest time in copy/offer skill (point 6) — since a tiny agency can't outspend competitors, the leverage point is a sharper offer and message, not ad budget.

## Do not copy

- Any reference to spending "$500,000" on learning or running "a million bucks a month" in ads — not replicable for a bootstrapped 3-person agency.
- "Buy up all the customers in the marketplace" / outspending competitors — assumes large ad budgets and credit lines ("chuck it on Amex") that a tiny agency won't have.
- Claims-heavy framing ("$7.8 billion," "200 million") used as authority/proof — don't invent or borrow stats the agency hasn't earned.
- Encouraging deliberate polarization/offending people to "spark debate" for algorithm reach — risky for a small agency's reputation and client trust.
- The assumption of an in-house media buying/sales team and large-scale paid traffic infrastructure — not available to a 3-person shop relying on cold outreach and organic content.
