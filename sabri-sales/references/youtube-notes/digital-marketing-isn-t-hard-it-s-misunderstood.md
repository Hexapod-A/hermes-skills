## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Every marketing channel is playing one of two games: demand capture (reaching people already searching for a solution) or demand generation (pushing a message to people who aren't yet looking). Pick one channel, master it completely instead of spreading thin across many, and recognize that converting attention (not getting more attention) is usually the real bottleneck.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **Coliseum keywords (Hack 1 — demand capture via Google Ads):** Find the 4-5 keywords that make up ~80% of search volume in your niche using the Google Ads search-term tool; build one deep standalone landing page per keyword; use exact match only, never broad/phrase, never PMax/AI campaign types.
2. **AI land grab / SEO (Hack 2):** Run a small paid campaign first to find which keywords actually convert, then build human-written, deeply comprehensive pages for those terms plus all related "people also ask" questions; stack trust signals (Google Business Profile, backlinks, press mentions).
3. **Dog-whistle copy + broad targeting (Hack 3 — demand generation via Meta):** Write long-form (300-600 word), hyper-specific copy that only your ideal prospect would read; let the algorithm's broad targeting (no interests/age/gender) do the work instead of manual interest-stacking.
4. **Hyperdopamine ad:** Combine one big specific benefit with "burning intrigue" (an open loop) in the ad; the ad's only job is to earn the click, not to sell.
5. **Native, not ad-looking creative:** Use raw phone photos/video, lowercase informal copy; never make the ad look like an ad.
6. **Google sniff test (Hack 4 — organic content):** Instead of posting everywhere constantly, map the 3-4 steps a prospect takes from where they are to their desired outcome and create one deep, evergreen pillar piece per step so prospects who Google you find proof you're legit.
7. **Planting an orchard (Hack 5 — email):** Make every email look personal (lowercase, plain text, human sender), keep it 80% value / 20% pitch, so it lands in primary inbox and compounds trust.
8. **SMS as top-of-mind, not spam (Hack 6):** Keep texts short, casual, lowercase, pointing to a video/VSL rather than pitching in the text itself.
9. **Affiliate/creator army (Hack 7):** Recruit 10-20 micro-influencers (sub-10k followers) to run satellite content pages for commission, then take their organically-proven content and run it as paid ads to their warm audience.
10. **Conversion rate optimization (Hack 8):** Fix headline first (it decides scroll vs. bounce), then test price (for ecommerce) or strengthen the lead-gen offer so it has standalone value, then stack proof (reviews, screenshots, before/afters) to overcome a "trust recession."

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Identify the 4-5 "coliseum" search terms small-business owners actually use (e.g., "automate invoicing for small business," "AI receptionist for [industry]") and build one deep landing page per term before running any search ads.
- Treat cold email and cold DM outreach like the "personal email" rule: write in plain text, lowercase-casual tone, no templated brand design, and lead with value (a quick audit insight, a relevant workflow teardown) before any pitch — aim for roughly 80/20 value-to-ask.
- Apply the "sniff test": before scaling outbound, have 3-4 solid pieces of content (case study, process explainer, before/after workflow) that answer "is this agency legit?" since prospects will Google you after a cold touch.
- For landing pages/VSLs, prioritize the headline and a real offer (e.g., a genuinely useful free automation audit) over design polish — make the free audit valuable enough that it would stand alone even if they never buy the build.
- Use proof over claims: real before/after screenshots of automated workflows, time saved, client testimonials — never invent stats or urgency not grounded in actual results.
- Pick one acquisition channel (e.g., cold email) and go deep before adding X, LinkedIn, and video simultaneously — avoid the "20 plates" trap for a 3-person team.
- If running any paid ads to test demand, make creative look native (founder talking into a phone camera) rather than polished agency-style ads, and let the targeting stay broad rather than over-niching on interests.
- Price-test the automation build/audit offer rather than guessing — a lower-friction entry price for an audit could increase conversion to the full build more than a single fixed high price.

## Do not copy

- The billion-dollar/2,000-funnels credibility claims — a 3-person agency has no comparable proof points and shouldn't fabricate scale.
- Heavy ad-budget tactics requiring real spend to "find keywords" (spending "a couple hundred, if not a couple thousand") before doing SEO — not viable on a tiny budget.
- Building a 10-20 person micro-influencer army (Hack 7) — requires management bandwidth and commission budget a 3-person shop likely doesn't have.
- References to Shark Tank fame and existing audience size that make his organic/content advice (e.g., "I was already doing $10M before YouTube") non-transferable to a cold-start agency.
- Any implication that these channels scale the same without his existing list, brand recognition, or media appearances that create instant trust.
