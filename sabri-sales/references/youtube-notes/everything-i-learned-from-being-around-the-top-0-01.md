## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby distills seven operating principles he observed from billionaires and top performers (Mark Cuban, Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos, and unnamed mentors): relentless work ethic, speed, ruthless prioritization, delegation, ownership, risk tolerance, and long time horizons. The throughline is that "luck" is manufactured by habits anyone can copy, not innate talent or birthright.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **The King's Audit** — For one week, log what you do in every 15-minute increment on a notepad. At week's end, list your activities on the left, your goals on the right, and cut anything that doesn't support the goals.
2. **80/20 decision rule** — Spend under 30 minutes gathering 80% of the info needed to decide; don't wait days/weeks for the last 20%. Act on ideas immediately (e.g., send a voice note the moment a task comes to mind) to close "open tabs" in your head.
3. **Default to No** — Set 1-3 max priorities per year (ideally one). Run every request through: "Does this support my priority?" If not, it's an automatic no — say it directly instead of "let me think about it."
4. **Delegate or Stay Small** — Calculate your effective hourly rate (profit or income ÷ hours worked). Revisit your King's Audit list and delegate any task you can pay someone less than that rate to do.
5. **Extreme Ownership post-mortem** — When something breaks, ask "How did I create this?" Trace it to a missing system, then build a fail-safe so it can't recur — don't blame staff or circumstance.
6. **Risk-the-downside exercise** — Before a scary decision, write down the actual worst-case outcome on paper; it's usually less catastrophic than imagined, which clarifies the risk/upside trade-off.
7. **Decade Thinking — Three Questions** — Ask: (1) Where do I want to be in 10 years? (2) What will it take to get there? (3) What am I willing to sacrifice to achieve it?

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Run your own King's Audit for a week before claiming you need more leads — you may be burning hours on non-sales tasks that could be delegated or automated.
- Apply "default to no": pick 1-3 priorities (e.g., cold outreach volume, one niche, one offer) and decline distractions like custom one-off builds that don't fit your ICP.
- Use the 80% decision rule when qualifying prospects or scoping builds — don't stall a proposal waiting for perfect information; get enough to move and adjust.
- When a build breaks or a client is unhappy, do the "how did I create this" post-mortem publicly in your process — build a checklist/system fix rather than blaming the client or the tool, and mention this ownership approach in sales conversations as a differentiator.
- Delegate low-value tasks (research, follow-ups, scheduling) to your own automations or VAs once you calculate your effective hourly rate — practice what you sell.
- Think in years, not days, about your agency's positioning: resist chasing every shiny automation trend; pick a long-horizon niche and stick with unglamorous outreach consistently.
- Speed matters in sales too — respond to inbound DM/email replies same-day; "money loves speed" applies directly to lead follow-up speed, a legitimate, provable lever (not hype).
- Take calculated risk honestly: invest in a real portfolio build or case study before you have paying clients, rather than claiming fake results — the risk is time/effort, not fabricated urgency.

## Do not copy

- The billionaire-proximity examples (Shark Tank, private jets, $10.2B in client sales, Elon/Bezos) rely on his fame, network access, and past ad spend — not replicable proof points for a small agency.
- His "$10.2 billion in sales" and "tens of thousands of books sold" claims are his own unverified figures; don't borrow or imply similar numbers without your own evidence.
- The implication that extreme personal sacrifice (16.5-hour days for 3+ years) is the only path — fine as a work-ethic point, but don't market burnout as a required or unique differentiator.
- Decade-thinking and big risk-taking (mortgaging everything into a build) sound good in a motivational video but shouldn't be repackaged as sales urgency tactics aimed at prospects.
- Nothing here is about honest outreach copy, offer design, or proof-building for cold audiences — it's internal operating philosophy, not a marketing/sales script to copy verbatim into outbound messaging.
