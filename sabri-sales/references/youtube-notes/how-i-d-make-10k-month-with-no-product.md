## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Every business has unused waste — dead leads, unrepurposed content, an underleveraged following, or disqualified leads — that it's already written off. Sabri Suby calls this the "sawdust method": you offer to monetize that waste for free (no upfront payment, just a revenue share), which makes it an almost risk-free "easiest yes" for the business owner, letting you start an agency with no product, audience, or capital.

## Frameworks and steps (numbered)

1. **The Sawdust Method (naming):** Identify an asset a business is throwing away (dead leads, old content, unused followers, disqualified leads) — this is their "sawdust" — and turn it into a profit center, same as sawmills selling sawdust waste as particle board.
2. **Pick your sawdust type:** (a) dead/unfollowed-up leads, (b) long-form content not repurposed, (c) social following not being monetized via DM outreach, (d) disqualified leads a premium business can't service.
3. **Pitch as zero-downside:** Frame the offer as "you're already doing nothing with this, so I only take a cut of revenue you wouldn't have gotten anyway" — removes the need for strong selling skill.
4. **Start with rev-share, not retainer:** Offer pure commission (10–30%, or 50% on resold leads) with no upfront payment so there's no risk for the business owner, maximizing yes-rate.
5. **Prospect by hand first:** Pick 10 creators/businesses you already know (or the first 10 ads you see in your feed) and DM/email them directly with a specific, researched pitch.
6. **Build proof, then upgrade terms:** Once you deliver and have case studies, shift the offer structure to a hybrid — a small retainer plus a percentage of upside.
7. **Graduate to paid acquisition:** Once cash-flowing from manual outreach, reinvest profits into paid ads to scale outreach volume beyond one-to-one cold contact.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Identify a prospect's "sawdust": old CRM leads never followed up, unused content archive, manual processes nobody has automated — pitch automating the follow-up or workflow instead of selling a generic "automation package."
- Offer a no-upfront-risk pilot: build a small automation (e.g., lead reactivation sequence) for free or cheap, take a cut of recovered revenue or a flat fee only after it demonstrably works — mirrors his rev-share zero-downside framing, but keep numbers honest (no invented recovery %).
- Use direct, specific outreach: research the business's actual dead-lead pile, content backlog, or manual process before DMing/emailing — specificity replaces persuasion skill.
- Start small and manual (cold email/DM to a short, real list) before any paid ad spend — matches his "10 people, one yes" bootstrap approach, appropriate for a tiny agency with no budget.
- Build real case studies from these pilot projects before asking for bigger retainers — let documented outcomes (not hype) justify moving to retainer/hybrid pricing.
- Position automation builds as "recovering value you're already losing" (e.g., unactioned leads, manual repetitive tasks) rather than as a speculative new initiative — lowers the perceived risk for a skeptical SMB owner.
- Avoid overclaiming stats like "50-60% of leads die" — instead ask the prospect directly what their actual follow-up/drop-off rate is and use their own number in the pitch.
- Once a few rev-share or pilot clients prove the model, transition honestly to flat project fees or retainers based on real delivered results, not manufactured urgency.

## Do not copy

- The specific "50–60% of leads go to the graveyard" statistic — presented as fact with no sourcing; don't repeat unverified numbers to prospects.
- The implication that virtually any business will say yes to a stranger's rev-share pitch — in reality, trust, credibility, and outreach volume matter more than the framing alone, especially without his audience/reputation.
- Any assumption of scale via "move into paid ads to hit $100k/month" — that step depends on capital and ad expertise this tiny agency likely doesn't have yet; stick to organic cold outreach.
- His casual tone implying this requires no sales skill — cold outreach to SMB owners for automation work still requires real discovery, trust-building, and follow-up, not just one repurposed script.
- Borrowing his brand authority (large audience, established agency) to make offers seem credible — a new agency must substitute real case studies/pilots for his implicit credibility.
