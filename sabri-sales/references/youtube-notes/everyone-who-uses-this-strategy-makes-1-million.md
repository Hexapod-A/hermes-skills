## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sabri Suby's funnel is built around a "high-value content offer" (HVCO) — a piece of content so good that competitors would charge for it — used to get cold prospects to raise their hand and give contact details. From there, a multi-channel follow-up system (SMS, calls, email, VSL) converts opt-ins into booked calls, and the sales call itself is restructured to deliver real value upfront instead of running a generic pitch. He claims this exact system has created "108 millionaires" across "a thousand different niches."

## Frameworks and steps (numbered)

1. **High-Value Content Offer (HVCO)** — Give away something competitors would charge for (swipe file, cheat sheet, guide), not a flimsy "5-page brochure" lead magnet, in exchange for contact details [0:00].
2. **Make it specific, not vague** — Target a narrow outcome for a narrow person (e.g., "$100/day to $100k/day") instead of generic "5 ways to grow your business" content; keep it 20-70 pages with lots of images [2:05].
3. **Multiple HVCOs per vertical** — At scale, run a different content offer for every service line you sell, not just one [3:11].
4. **Landing page design** — Headline + subheadline should expand on each other (not repeat); show a visual mockup of the report/guide; add light social proof and audience-specific visual cues (flag, map, testimonial) [6:20].
5. **Fill the funnel (traffic)** — Favor volume of static ads over video ads for cheaper CPMs and faster iteration; disguise ads as native content to raise dwell time and CTR [8:26].
6. **Instant multi-channel follow-up** — Within 30 seconds of opt-in, send a casual, lower-case SMS; also call immediately, double-dial if no answer, leave a voicemail, and send an email — cover every channel [10:29].
7. **Dual-path routing** — Route opt-ins to both a VSL (direct offer) and outbound dialing simultaneously; relying on inbound-only roughly doubles customer acquisition cost [11:30].
8. **Scale with AI** — At higher volume, feed transcribed SMS/call/email logs into an AI chatbot that sorts prospects by problem/service and routes them to a custom VSL for a personalized pitch [12:32].
9. **The sales call structure** — ~50% discovery (where they are, what they've tried, where they want to go), ~20-30% delivering a "micro" piece of value live (audit a funnel, review a stat), ~20% pitch that bridges current state to desired state [14:36].
10. **Team scaling** — Start solo (manual SMS/calls), then hire "setter-closers" who book and close their own appointments, then graduate to separate dedicated setter and closer teams [16:41].

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Build a genuinely useful HVCO (e.g., "5 Automations Every [niche] Business Should Have" checklist, or a workflow audit template) that a prospect would normally have to pay a consultant for — not a thin PDF about your company.
- Make the offer narrow and specific to the ICP (e.g., "Cut invoice processing from 3 hours to 10 minutes" for bookkeepers) rather than generic "AI can help your business" messaging.
- On the landing page/VSL, show a visual mockup of what they'll get (sample audit report screenshot) so the value is concrete before they opt in.
- Follow up fast and through multiple honest channels (email + DM + short follow-up video) without fabricating urgency — speed and persistence, not fake scarcity.
- On discovery/strategy calls, spend most of the time understanding their actual current process and pain points before pitching anything.
- Deliver a small piece of real value live on the call — e.g., map out one automation or point out one obvious time-sink in their workflow — to prove capability through action, not claims.
- As volume grows, use AI to triage inbound leads (categorize by problem/industry) and route them to relevant, honestly-written case studies or VSLs — but only with real client results, not invented numbers.

## Do not copy

- His claimed scale figures ("$7.8 billion in sales," "108 millionaires," "$300 million in ad testing") depend on years of agency fame, a large ad budget, and brand recognition — not reproducible for a small agency.
- The paid traffic engine (hundreds of static Meta ads, large CPM budgets) requires significant ad spend a tiny agency likely doesn't have; don't assume you can "fill the funnel" the same way without capital.
- Comparisons to billion-dollar companies (Fisher Investments) are illustrative, not a benchmark — don't borrow their scale as proof your funnel should produce similar numbers.
- His own tool plug (getconcong.ai) and proprietary swipe library are part of his monetization, not a technique to copy or necessary for the funnel to work.
- The "guarantee to beat any competitor by 30%" is a specific high-stakes offer backed by his agency's resources/track record — don't make guarantees you can't actually back.
