## Core idea (2-3 sentences)

Sales is won or lost before the "close" — in the diagnosis, your tone/conviction, and your willingness to do unglamorous fundamentals (follow-up, role-play, tracking inputs) better and longer than competitors. There are no secret closing lines; value exceeding price, delivered with conviction, is what makes deals happen.

## Frameworks and steps

1. **Diagnosis before prescription ("sell like a doctor")**: Spend ~80% of the call listening, extract exact words for their problem, what they've tried, what worked/didn't — the deal closes at discovery, not at closing.
2. **One feeds two**: After diagnosis, give a short (≤2 min) live demonstration of the solution using the prospect's exact language, visually if possible (dashboards, live analysis) — don't do a 15-minute full presentation.
3. **Push for a decision, not "maybe"**: State your intentions upfront; the goal is yes or no — "maybe"/"let me think about it" kills more deals than "no."
4. **Agree first, then redirect ("sales aikido")**: Never argue or try to prove a prospect wrong; agree with whatever they say, then pivot back to diagnosis/value.
5. **Find the silent objection**: Surface objections ("I need to think about it") are smokescreens — ask directly what specifically they need to think about, then use a 1–10 scale to locate real hesitation and solve for it.
6. **Role-play daily, starting from the hardest objection**: Train twice a day, beginning each round with the worst possible objection ("deep water" start), not on live prospects.
7. **Build conviction deliberately**: Qualify thoroughly, review testimonials/past wins ("parade of evidence"), and remember your best customers had the same objections as skeptics but acted anyway.
8. **Assign a cost to inaction**: Make explicit what staying the same costs them emotionally/financially — this is usually bigger than your price.
9. **Three-tier pricing + "mini-bomb" + silence**: Always present high/mid/low packages, summarize all value before stating price (mini-bomb), state the price, then stay silent and let them justify it internally.
10. **Track leading indicators daily, not outcomes monthly**: Monitor activity inputs (talk time, number of calls/sets) daily so you can course-correct before a bad month happens.
11. **Systematize follow-up**: Map your average sales cycle length and touchpoints, then triple the touchpoint count, scheduling exact days/channels — "value-added follow-up" referencing what the prospect already told you.

## Use for an AI automation agency

- Before pitching any build, run a real discovery call: ask what manual processes cost them in hours/errors, what they've already tried (Zapier, VAs, other agencies), and reflect their exact words back when describing the automation.
- Use a short live demo (one feeds two) — e.g., show a live n8n/Zapier workflow solving one piece of their stated problem in a couple minutes, rather than a long feature-dump deck.
- Replace vague "I'll think about it" with a direct ask: "On a scale of 1–10, how ready are you to move forward?" to surface the real objection (budget, trust in AI, past bad vendor experience).
- Quantify cost of inaction honestly — real hours/week spent on manual tasks they told you about, not invented ROI stats — and let them do the math.
- Offer three tiers (e.g., audit-only, single-workflow build, full automation package) instead of one take-it-or-leave-it price.
- Track your own leading indicators (cold emails sent, DMs sent, calls booked) daily instead of waiting to see if the month "feels" good — adjust outreach volume before pipeline dries up.
- Build a genuine follow-up sequence mapped to your real sales cycle length, where each touchpoint adds value (e.g., "I noticed your intake form could save 3 hours/week — here's a quick Loom") rather than "just checking in."
- When asked "does this actually work," rely on real client results/testimonials you've collected, not fabricated case studies or inflated claims.

## Do not copy

- His claimed revenue figures ($300M, $7.8B, 108+ millionaires) are unverifiable self-promotion and irrelevant to teaching the method — don't borrow the numbers or the authority-by-scale framing.
- "Raise your prices, thank me later" advice assumes an established brand/portfolio and high inbound demand; a new automation agency can't use pricing-as-positioning the same way without proof to back it.
- The large sales-team infrastructure (twice-daily role-play sessions, dedicated sales floors, Slack "victory channels," CRM dashboards with custom KPIs) assumes a multi-person sales org — a solo/tiny team should adapt the principles (practice objection handling, track activity) at much smaller scale, not copy the apparatus.
- Aggressive "push them to yes/no, no maybes" framing works well in his high-volume, well-resourced sales environment; a small agency should still avoid manufacturing false urgency or pressuring prospects who have legitimate reasons to delay.
