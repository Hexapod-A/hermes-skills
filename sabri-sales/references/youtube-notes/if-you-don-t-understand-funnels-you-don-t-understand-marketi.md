## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
Sabri Suby ranks eight client-getting funnels from F-tier to S-tier based on lead quality, scalability, and complexity. His central thesis: the "gold standard" funnels pre-educate and pre-qualify prospects *before* a sales call using a Video Sales Letter (VSL), so reps only talk to people who already understand the offer — this lowers CAC and raises ROAS. The more friction and consumption you require before a prospect reaches your sales team, the higher-quality (but slower) the lead.

## Frameworks and steps (numbered)
1. **Facebook Lead Ads (F-tier):** Instant on-platform form, zero friction, cheapest leads — but lowest intent; only works if called within 60 seconds.
2. **Landing Page + Contact Form (C-tier):** Headline → VSL → long-form copy → contact form; low build cost, no qualification mechanism, caps around $100–200K/mo.
3. **Instagram DM Funnel (C-tier):** Valuable content ad → prompts DM → conversational qualifying sequence → book a call; requires humans manually in DMs to scale, caps ~$500K/mo.
4. **YouTube Organic Funnel (F-tier as a starter):** Content with link in description; works only if you already have an audience; becomes a "force multiplier" (parallel "Google sniff test" trust-check), not a primary scaling channel.
5. **Low-Ticket Community Funnel (D-tier):** Community social proof → upsell to high-ticket; avoid unless you have a killer sub-4-hour course; attracts low-budget members, poor margins for paid ads.
6. **JIT (Just-In-Time) Webinar Funnel (A-tier for ceiling):** Ads → low-ticket/entry offer webinar used to "liquidate ad spend" → activation call is a disguised pitch → setters upsell high-ticket backend. Actually 5 funnels duct-taped together; highest revenue ceiling ($10M+/mo) but highest complexity.
7. **Ungated VSL Booker-Call Funnel (A-tier):** VSL page → direct call booking (no forced watch) → add a **bridge page** (pre-call page with VSL + demo/mechanism video + proof) to lift "selling freight" before the call. Cheapest cost-per-booked-call; caps near $3M/mo; needs strong copywriting and reminder sequences.
8. **Gated VSL Book-a-Call Funnel ("the goat funnel," S-tier):** Capture email + phone + pixel ("land, sea, and sky") → setters call immediately after opt-in referencing the training watched → use CRM tags to track % of VSL consumed → only book calls for those who finished the VSL → expect 3–5 calls to close for B2B $5K+ offers (not one-call close). Halves CAC, doubles ROAS; highest probability path to 8 figures.

## Use for an AI automation agency
- Build a short, honest VSL (video walkthrough of your automation process/results) instead of a hard "book now" pitch — prove you can solve the problem inside the video itself before asking for the call.
- Use a **bridge/pre-call page** after someone books: short demo video showing the actual automation mechanism (not pricing) plus real client examples, so discovery calls start at "does this fit my business" not "what do you even do."
- Capture email + phone (not just a DM or form) so you have multiple channels to follow up — but only follow up with real information, not fabricated urgency.
- Track actual content/VSL watch completion (if using email/landing tools) and prioritize outreach — call/DM the ones who engaged most first; this is just honest lead scoring, not manipulation.
- For small-ticket automation audits (under ~$5K), aim for a one-call structure; for larger builds/retainers, plan for multiple calls — set that expectation with the prospect up front rather than forcing a fake one-call close.
- Avoid relying solely on cold low-friction channels (e.g., a single cold DM blast) as your only funnel — pair it with some educational content (a short video/post) so the prospect isn't cold when they hit a call.
- Resist building a "community" or cheap trial product as a proxy for your real paid offer — for a tiny agency, directly pitching the real automation build/audit is more efficient than funneling people through free, low-commitment layers.
- Use real client results and a clear explanation of your process as your "proof," not manufactured scarcity or inflated claims — matches his point that skepticism is lowered by genuinely helpful content, not hype.

## Do not copy
- His implied ad budgets and team size (setters, closers, CRM infrastructure, "ninja tech") assume dedicated sales teams and paid traffic at scale — not realistic for a 3-person agency doing cold outreach.
- The JIT webinar funnel strategy of "liquidating ad spend" requires real ad budget and backend upsell infrastructure most tiny agencies don't have.
- Revenue figures like "$10.2 billion," "$50 million plus clients," "2,000 funnels" are his personal credibility claims — don't borrow these as evidence for your own business.
- The assumption of large volumes of inbound leads (tens of thousands/month) for A/B testing VSL watch-time thresholds doesn't apply at small-agency lead volumes.
- Hiring and training dedicated sales teams to "wrestle leads to the ground" within 60 seconds isn't feasible or necessary for a founder-led outbound motion.
