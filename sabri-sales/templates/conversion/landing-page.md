# Conversion module: landing page / offer page

Sabri says every sales message must answer 9 questions, in order, before the reader acts:
1. Is this for me? 2. Why pay attention now? 3. Is this the exact fix I need? 4. What is new about it?
5. What is the proof? 6. How does it work? 7. What do others say? 8. What guarantee do I have? 9. What do you want me to do next?

His best funnel for a service like ours is the "ungated VSL, book a call" page: headline, short video, the copy below it, and a booking form. Add a "bridge page" after booking (a short video + proof) so people show up ready.

## Rules

- The page answers the 9 questions. Each section has a job. Cut any section that has no job.
- One CTA, repeated: "Book a free automation audit".
- Answer question 7 (what others say) ONLY with real testimonials John gives you. If there are none, skip the section. Never invent one.
- Real scarcity only: "up to 10 audits a week" and why.
- Plain words. Short sentences. Mobile first: most owners will read this on a phone.

## Sabri's page order (his "17-step selling system", from his landing-page course)

Call out the reader → big promise headline → sub-headline → intrigue → the problem → the solution → credentials → benefits, not features → proof → the offer → bonuses → build value → price (skip: John sets prices, never in this skill) → real scarcity → guarantee → clear CTA → P.S. that restates the deal.
The section map below follows this order in a shorter form for a phone screen.

More rules from the same course:
- Headline formula: specific person + dream outcome + named method + less effort or a guarantee. Leave out any time frame John has not confirmed.
- Sub-headlines are hooks. Reading only the headline and sub-headlines should tell the whole story.
- Proof must be louder than claims. Show the real workflow, the plan, a screen recording. Delete any claim you can't prove.
- Name the method (his "hero mechanism"): say why other fixes fail, why ours works, and give it a name. Draft name: "Boring Task First" (John to approve).
- "Cheese, not whiskers": a cold visitor raises a hand for free value (the audit and a plan they keep), not for "book a call" or "get a quote".
- Write like you talk: record John pitching a stranger for half an hour, transcribe it, and use his own words as raw copy.
- Build the FAQ from real buyers: what almost stopped them, what they tried before. Biggest objection first.
- Match the page to the reader: cold readers need more teaching and a longer page; warm readers need less.

## Section map

```text
1. Headline: {Pain or outcome for the dream buyer}            -> Q1, Q3
2. Sub-headline: who it is for + what they get              -> Q1
3. Short video (VSL) + CTA button                           -> Q2-Q6
4. The problem: the boring tasks, in the reader's words      -> Q2
5. How it works: audit -> plan -> build -> care              -> Q6
6. What is new: we map the task first, then pick the tool    -> Q4
7. Proof: 3+ years, 23+ systems, 450+ hours, 12 industries   -> Q5
8. What clients say: real quotes only, or skip               -> Q7
9. The three guarantees                                      -> Q8
10. Real limit: up to 10 audits a week, and why              -> Q2
11. CTA: Book a free automation audit                        -> Q9
12. FAQ: cost, time, tools, "will it break?"                 -> objections
```

```copy

We build AI automations that take the boring, repeated work off your team. They get time back for the work that needs a person.

[Button] Book a free automation audit

Sound familiar?
You copy the same data from one app to another.
You chase invoices by hand.
You answer the same customer questions all day.

Plan. You get a written plan: what to automate first, and what it will do. You keep it.
Build. We build the system and connect it to the tools you already use.
Care. We look after it for 6 months after launch.

Why we start with the boring tasks
A chatbot looks smart. But most lost hours hide in copy-paste work and slow replies. We fix those first, because they save the most time and break the least.

Proof
3+ years building automations. 23+ systems built. 450+ hours saved for clients. Clients in 12 industries.

You don't carry the risk
Pay half after you see it work. You pay 50% to start, and the other 50% only after you see the system working in your business.
Audit fee credit. If you go ahead after a paid audit, the audit fee comes off the build.
6 months of care. After launch, if something we built breaks, we fix it. Fixes and upkeep, not new features.

Why only 10 audits a week
A builder does each audit, not a salesperson. That is how you get a real plan, not a sales pitch. So we do up to 10 audits a week.

[Button] Book a free automation audit
```

## Filled example: FAQ block

Do I need to understand AI?

Usually not. We connect the tools you already use.

You get 6 months of fixes and upkeep after launch. If something we built breaks, we fix it.

The first audit call is free. Book a free automation audit and we will tell you what a build would involve before you pay anything.
